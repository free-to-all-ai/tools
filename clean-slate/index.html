Michigan Clean Slate Expungement Check

Michigan can expunge many old convictions. The law calls it a "set aside." Under the Clean Slate law, some convictions clear automatically after 7 or 10 years. Others clear if you apply to the court that convicted you. List your convictions and dates below to see whether each one may be eligible for expungement, which route fits, and when.

This is information, not legal advice. It can only tell you what you may qualify for. A judge decides every application, and the State Police run the automatic clearing. Free legal help is listed at the bottom. Nothing you type here leaves this page or gets saved.

Which crimes count as "assaultive" or "serious misdemeanors"?

These two groups matter because they can't clear automatically, they have a longer wait when you apply, and only 2 assaultive convictions can ever be cleared.

  • Assaultive crimes include assault or battery, domestic violence, aggravated assault, resisting or obstructing police, home invasion, unarmed robbery, kidnapping, unlawful imprisonment, stalking, child abuse, criminal sexual conduct, and other violent felonies. (MCL 780.621(4)(a) and 770.9a)
  • Serious misdemeanors also include breaking and entering or illegal entry, indecent exposure, aiming or firing a gun at someone, contributing to the delinquency of a minor, leaving the scene of an injury crash, drunk driving that caused a crash with damage or injury, selling alcohol to a minor who then got hurt, and a charge that started as one of these and was pleaded down. (MCL 780.811)

Free help clearing your record

  • Michigan Legal Help: Do-It-Yourself Expungement asks you questions and fills out the court forms for free.
  • Safe & Just Michigan has a free self-assessment and runs free expungement fairs around the state, where volunteer lawyers help you apply.
  • Michigan Attorney General: Expungement Assistance lists upcoming expungement fairs and answers common questions. Phone 517-335-7622.
  • Detroit residents can get free help from the city's Project Clean Slate.
  • Michigan Legal Help: Guide to Legal Help and the State Bar of Michigan legal aid directory find free legal aid near you.
  • If something should have cleared automatically but still shows, email the State Police at MSP-CRD-CSAAUTOMATIC@michigan.gov or call their criminal history line at 517-241-0606. If a court moves to bring back a cleared conviction, Michigan Legal Help points people to the Counsel & Advocacy Law Line at 1-888-783-8190.

How this works. The rules come from Michigan's set aside law, 1965 PA 213 (MCL 780.621 to 780.624), including the 2020 Clean Slate amendments and the first-OWI amendments that took effect in 2022. The text was checked on legislature.mi.gov, which shows the law complete through 2026 PA 114, on October 31, 2026. Definitions come from MCL 780.811 (serious misdemeanor) and MCL 770.9a and 791.236 (assaultive and violent crimes). Steps, fees, and contacts come from the Michigan State Police, the Attorney General, court form MC 227 (revised March 2025), and Michigan Legal Help, all checked the same day.

Waiting periods are counted in whole years from the dates you enter. For new convictions, this page uses the sentencing date, which usually comes a few weeks after the conviction. Green cards are what the law says directly. Amber cards depend on facts this page can't check, or on a part of the law that people read in different ways. Red cards mean get free legal help before you go further.

This page doesn't cover juvenile records, convictions from other states or federal court, pardons, or arrests and charges that never led to a conviction. A case dismissed under a deferral program like HYTA or MCL 333.7411 isn't a conviction, but it counts as a misdemeanor when a court checks whether you can apply, and it must be listed on your application.
