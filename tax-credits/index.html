Michigan Tax Credits You Might Be Missing

Lots of working families, renters, and seniors in Michigan qualify for tax credits they never claim. Answer a few questions to estimate four of them: the federal Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC), Michigan's Working Families Tax Credit (the Michigan earned income tax credit), the Homestead Property Tax Credit for homeowners and renters, and the Home Heating Credit. Some of this money comes to you even if you owe no tax.

This is tax information, not tax advice. It's an estimate built from the rules and numbers listed at the bottom of this page. The IRS and the Michigan Department of Treasury decide what you actually get. Nothing you type here leaves this page or gets saved.

    2. Missed a year? Some are still open

    If you didn't file, or filed without a credit you qualified for, you can usually still claim it for a few years. You file that year's return (or an amended one). These dates are the last day to claim a refund.

    4. Get them filed for free

    • Free tax help near you: call 2-1-1, text 898211, or go to MichiganFreeTaxHelp.org. Trained volunteers file your federal and Michigan returns and the credit forms at no cost.
    • IRS volunteer sites (VITA and TCE): use the IRS site finder or call 800-906-9887. For AARP Tax-Aide, which helps people of any age, call 888-227-7669 from January to April.
    • Do it yourself online: IRS Free File reopens in January 2027. For the 2026 filing season, its guided software was free with income of $95,000 or less. Michigan lets you file your state return free through Michigan Treasury eServices.
    • Paper forms are at most public libraries and county MDHHS offices.
    • If you pay a preparer, they have to sign your return and give their preparer number. Never sign a blank return.
    What to bring
    • Photo ID, and Social Security cards for you, your spouse, and each child.
    • W-2s and 1099s, including 1099-NEC or 1099-K for gig work, plus your records of self-employment income and expenses.
    • Social Security or SSI statements (SSA-1099), unemployment (1099-G), and pension forms (1099-R).
    • MDHHS Client Annual Statement (DHS-1241), and the Friend of the Court statement (FEN-851) if you get child support through the court.
    • Owners: both property tax bills for the year (summer and winter) and your taxable value.
    • Renters: your lease or your landlord's name and address, and the rent you paid each month.
    • Heating bills or fuel receipts from November 1 to October 31.
    • Health insurance premiums you paid, and Form 1095-A if you had a Marketplace plan.
    • Your bank's routing number and your account number, for direct deposit.
    • Last year's tax returns, if you filed.

    Federal EITC. The 2026 amounts come from IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32, section 4.06, and the 2025 amounts from Revenue Procedure 2024-40, section 3.06, which match the IRS EITC tables. The phase-in and phase-out rates are in 26 U.S.C. 32(b). Who qualifies follows the IRS pages on who qualifies, qualifying children, and claiming earlier years. For a full check, use the IRS EITC Assistant.

    Michigan. The Working Families Tax Credit is 30% of the federal EITC under MCL 206.272 (Treasury's EITC page). The Homestead Property Tax Credit follows MCL 206.520, Treasury's 2025 limits, and the 2025 MI-1040CR instructions. The Home Heating Credit follows MCL 206.527a (last changed by 2025 PA 55), Treasury's 2025 Table A and Table B, its Home Heating Credit page, and the 2025 MI-1040CR-7 form. Treasury took that form down after the deadline, so the link is an archived copy.

    2026 Michigan numbers. As of October 31, 2026, Treasury hadn't posted 2026 limits for the Homestead or Home Heating credits. It says the 2026 Home Heating Credit form comes out in January 2027. Until then, 2026 Michigan estimates use the 2025 numbers and are marked as estimates. The federal 2026 EITC numbers are final.
