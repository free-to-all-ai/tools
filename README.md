# Free to All AI

**You can and must understand AI now.**

**Use the tools here: [free-to-all-ai.github.io/tools](https://free-to-all-ai.github.io/tools/)**

## Why "Free to All"

Three words are carved over the main entrance of the Boston Public Library: **FREE TO ALL**. Founded in 1848, it was the first large free municipal library in the United States. When its McKim Building opened in 1895, the promise went over the door. Anyone could walk in and use the most powerful information tools of the day, whoever they were and whatever they could pay. ([source](https://bpl.org/news/mckim-building-125th-anniversary))

AI is the most powerful information tool of our day. This project is that promise, rewritten for now.

## Why now

In 1974, Ted Nelson self-published *Computer Lib/Dream Machines* and told regular people that computers were too important to leave to the experts. Now the same is true of AI. The people in power are already using it. Everyone else should get to use it too.

This repo is a shelf of small, free tools for regular people. Each one does one job, gives you a checkable answer, and shows you where that answer comes from.

## How they work

- **One file each.** Every tool is a single `index.html`. Download it and open it in any browser, even offline.
- **Nothing leaves your device.** No accounts, no tracking, no servers. What you type stays on your screen.
- **Every answer cites its source.** Each card links to the law or data it came from and is labeled by how sure it is: what the law says directly, what depends on your facts, and when to get help now.
- **Information, not advice.** These tools explain your rights and the rules. They are not a lawyer, a doctor, or a tax preparer.

## Keeping them current

Laws and rates change. Every tool lists the date its sources were checked. Every tool also has a "See a mistake?" link at the bottom that opens a short report form here on GitHub (`.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/mistake.yml`). The Medicare Savings Program check, the hospital aid check, and the bill checker each show a good-through date near the top and switch to a warning once it passes. Those dates sit next to the numbers at the top of each script, so move them only after you update the numbers. The bill checker's rates come from CMS and change every January. To rebuild it, see `bill-checker/make.py`. The gig pay calculator's IRS mileage rates live in a short table near the top of its script. The IRS usually sets next year's rate in December. The shutoff check's poverty guideline numbers sit at the top of its script. HHS publishes new ones each January. The hospital aid check's poverty guideline and each hospital's rules sit at the top of its script. Hospitals revise their policies without notice, so recheck each one when you update it. The Medicare Savings Program check keeps its limits in one block at the top of its script. MDHHS updates the income limits each April 1 and the savings limits each January 1, and CMS sets the next year's Part B premium each fall. The tax credits check keeps its numbers in one block at the top of its script: add the IRS's EITC amounts for the next year when the IRS posts them in the fall, and Michigan's Homestead and Home Heating Credit numbers when Treasury posts them, usually in January. The Medicaid rides finder keeps every plan's ride number and booking notice in one list at the top of its script. Plans switch ride companies often (Molina moved to SafeRide on October 1, 2026), so recheck each plan's ride page and the state's plan ride list, linked in the tool, every few months. The medical debt credit report check keeps its dollar floor, waiting period, mailing addresses, and the status of the law in one block at the top of its script. The bureaus' medical debt rules are voluntary and can change without notice, so recheck each bureau's page every few months, and check whether Michigan SB 451 or HB 6073 has become law. The payday loan calculator keeps its limits in one block at the top of its script. The fee percentages are set in the statute and have not changed since 2005. DIFS sets the database fee with no fixed schedule ($2.40 since September 1, 2025), re-sets the returned check charge and repayment plan fee every five years (next by March 1, 2031), and posts a new annual report each October 31. The 28% credit union loan cap depends on an NCUA rate ceiling that runs through September 10, 2027. The small claims helper keeps the dollar limit, filing fees, and every day count in one block at the top of its script. The $7,000 limit has no scheduled increase in the law, so a change would come from a new act amending MCL 600.8401. The $5 electronic filing fee ends after February 28, 2031 unless the legislature extends it. The state rewrote the small claims forms in June 2026, so recheck the form names and the court fee table once a year. The eviction papers check keeps its day counts and form dates in one block at the top of its script. Recheck it once a year and whenever the Michigan Supreme Court amends Court Rule 4.201 or the state courts revise an eviction form. Three things in it are unsettled and need a look every few months: HUD's 30-day notice rule for public and subsidized housing (HUD moved to cancel it in February 2026, then put that on hold), whether Skidmore v Hayashi is appealed, and funding for Detroit's free eviction lawyers.

## Add your own

Found a wrong number, date, or link? [Report a mistake](https://github.com/free-to-all-ai/tools/issues/new?template=mistake.yml). Have a tool that helps people and has a checkable right answer? Open an issue or a pull request. The bar is simple: it cites its sources, it doesn't track anyone, and it tells people when they need a real professional.

So people can find a new tool: give its page a plain-language `<title>` and description in the words people actually search for, add a card to the homepage (`index.html`), add its address to `sitemap.xml`, add a 1200 by 630 link-preview image in `og/`, and copy the "See a mistake?" line from another tool's footer with your tool's name in the link.
