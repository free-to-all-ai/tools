Detroit Hospital Financial Assistance Check

Can't pay a hospital bill? Most big hospitals around Detroit have a financial assistance policy, often called charity care, that erases or cuts bills for people under set income limits. Enter your household size and income to see how the policies at Henry Ford, Corewell Health (Beaumont), Trinity Health, McLaren, the Detroit Medical Center (DMC), and Michigan Medicine would likely treat you. The old Ascension hospitals around Detroit are Henry Ford hospitals now, so they're here too.

This is information, not legal or financial advice. Only the hospital can approve you, after you apply. Results are estimates from each hospital's posted policy, checked on October 31, 2026. Nothing you type here leaves this page or gets saved.

How to apply

  1. Ask for the application now, even if you're not sure you qualify. Call the number on the hospital's card above or apply in its patient portal (MyChart at Henry Ford, Corewell, and Trinity). Nonprofit hospitals must take applications until at least 240 days after the first bill they send after your care (26 CFR 1.501(r)-1(b)(3)). At Henry Ford's former Ascension hospitals, applying late only helps with what you haven't paid yet.
  2. Gather proof of income. Most applications ask for your last federal tax return, W-2s, recent pay stubs or benefit letters, recent bank statements, and a photo ID. Henry Ford asks for 2 months of pay stubs and bank statements. Corewell asks for 3 months and wants the application back within 10 days. If you have no income, a letter from whoever supports you usually works.
  3. Apply for Medicaid too if you might qualify. Henry Ford, McLaren, and Corewell say they may require it before they decide. You can apply at MI Bridges, and hospital financial counselors can help.
  4. Send it, keep a copy, and ask them to pause collections. Once you apply during the 240 days, a nonprofit hospital has to stop collection actions until it decides (26 CFR 1.501(r)-6(c)).
  5. Get the answer in writing, and appeal a denial. Henry Ford decides within 30 days of a complete application and takes written appeals within 30 days of a denial. McLaren takes appeals within 30 calendar days at financialassistance@mclaren.org. Trinity gives at least 30 days to appeal. Corewell reviews denials at the next level up.
  6. If you already paid, ask for money back. If you qualify, a nonprofit hospital must refund what you paid above what you owe under its policy, unless it's under $5 (26 CFR 1.501(r)-6(c)(6)).
  7. Check the doctor bills separately. Doctors, labs, and ambulance companies that bill on their own may not follow the hospital's policy. Each hospital posts a list of who is covered. Ask each one that sends you a bill.

How this works. The tool divides your yearly household income by the 2026 federal poverty guideline for your household size. Then it checks that percent against each hospital system's posted policy, along with the policy's rules on insurance, where you live, savings, and very large bills. Green means the policy likely covers you. Amber means it might, depending on facts the hospital decides. Gray means your income is over the policy's limit, though large-bill rules or an appeal may still help.

Hospitals decide who counts in a household and what counts as income in slightly different ways, and some still use last year's poverty guideline until they update. Close to a limit? Apply anyway.
