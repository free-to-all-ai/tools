Unemployment Protest Deadline

Michigan's Unemployment Insurance Agency (UIA) denied your unemployment, cut it, or says you were overpaid. You can protest. Enter the mail date on your notice to see the last day UIA must receive your protest or appeal, where to send it, and what happens next. Then draft the protest letter.

This is information, not legal advice. The deadline is about when UIA gets your protest, not when you send it. If your notice shows an earlier date than this page, go by your notice. Nothing you type here leaves this page or gets saved.

Your protest letter

Fill in anything highlighted, then sign it. UIA says a faxed or mailed protest or appeal must be signed. Keep a copy and your proof of the date UIA got it. You can also use the state's own form, Form UIA 1733, which has a protest side and an appeal side.

2. Going to circuit court

An appeal from the Appeals Commission is filed in a circuit court, with court forms and a filing fee, so this page doesn't draft one. Free legal help is listed below. Bring the Commission's decision and the date it was mailed.

Free help

  • UIA Advocacy Program, 1-800-638-3994, option 2. A free advocate can help you at your first hearing with a judge. Call after you get your Notice of Hearing, at least two business days before the hearing. Some cases don't qualify.
  • Michigan Legal Help: Overview of Unemployment Benefits, and its Guide to Legal Help to find free legal aid near you.
  • State Bar of Michigan legal aid directory. Look for "employment" or "unemployment."
  • UIA customer service: 1-866-500-0017 (TTY 1-866-366-0004), Monday to Friday, 8 AM to 4:30 PM. Phones and offices are closed from 4:30 PM Monday, Nov. 2, 2026 until 8 AM Monday, Nov. 9.
  • MOAHR unemployment hearings: 313-456-2700, MOAHRUA@michigan.gov.
  • Unemployment Insurance Appeals Commission: 800-738-6372 or 517-284-9300.

How this works. Deadlines come from the Michigan Employment Security Act: MCL 421.32a (protests and appeals to a judge), 421.32 (monetary determinations), 421.33 (judge decisions), 421.34 (Appeals Commission), 421.38 (circuit court), 421.49 (weekends and legal holidays), and 421.62 (overpayments), plus MCL 435.101 (Michigan's legal holidays), MCL 8.6 (counting days), and Michigan Administrative Code R 421.270 (good cause). UIA's own rules come from its Protests and Appeals page, its Handbook for Unemployed Workers (July 2026), Form UIA 1733 (July 2026), its guide to appeals hearings, its 2026 law changes page, and its MiUI switchover page. All were checked against the official sources on October 31, 2026.

Days are calendar days, counted starting the day after the mail date. When day 30 lands on a Saturday, Sunday, or Michigan legal holiday, the law moves the deadline to the next day that isn't one. UIA counts a protest or appeal as filed when it has it in hand. A postmark doesn't count. Green cards are what the rule says directly. Amber cards depend on your facts. Red cards mean act now.
