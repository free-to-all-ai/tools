Free College in Michigan: Who Qualifies?

Michigan has four big programs that can make community college tuition free, and some help with a four-year degree. Michigan Reconnect is for adults 21 and older with no degree. The Michigan Achievement Scholarship, including its Community College Guarantee, is for recent high school graduates. TIP is for students who had Medicaid as kids. The Detroit Promise is for students who lived in Detroit and went to high school there.

Answer a few questions to see which ones you likely qualify for, which rule might stop you, and what to do next. Each answer links to the official page it comes from.

These rules change with each state budget. This year's budget (for the 2026-27 school year) lowered Reconnect's age from 25 to 21 and dropped the rule that Achievement Scholarship students must graduate from a Michigan high school. Everything here was checked against official pages on October 31, 2026.
This is information, not advice. Your college's financial aid office and the state make the final call. Nothing you type here leaves this page or gets saved.

3. Steps to claim it

These programs pay your college, not you, so most of the work is getting your paperwork to the college. Check things off as you go. Nothing is saved.

Free help

  • Your college's financial aid office applies all of these programs to your bill and works out what's left. Start there.
  • MI Student Aid (Achievement Scholarship, TIP, and Reconnect questions): 888-447-2687, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:45 p.m., or mistudentaid@michigan.gov.
  • Michigan Reconnect Navigators help for free with picking a program, the FAFSA, and registering. They hold online office hours Tuesdays 12 to 2 p.m. and Thursdays 5 to 7 p.m. See the Navigators page.
  • Detroit Promise: info@detroitpromise.com or its contact form. Students have to write in themselves, but can copy a parent.
  • Detroit Reconnect, run by the Detroit Regional Chamber, gives free coaching to adults 21 and older in Southeast Michigan, from finishing a GED to filling out the FAFSA. Detroit Reconnect page.
  • No diploma or GED yet? The state's free adult education programs are listed at Michigan.gov/LearnMoreEarnMore.

Things the official pages don't settle, and how this page handles them:

  • Michigan's Reconnect law still says 25. The 2026-27 budget lowered the age to 21, and the governor called the $25 million "ongoing." Bridge Michigan reported in August 2026 that advocates expect the lower age to last one year unless lawmakers change the law. This page uses 21 and flags it.
  • The Achievement Scholarship's 15-month start window comes with a chart for spring graduates (finish in spring, start by the fall of the next year). For other months, the state says to ask your financial aid office, so this page counts 15 months and labels it "maybe."
  • The Detroit Promise site says its application closed on July 1, 2026 while the program is updated, and doesn't say when it reopens. Its pages list the Path Back years as both 2013 to 2025 and 2013 to 2024. Its university GPA list and table disagree for the University of Olivet (2.5 or 3.0), and Siena Heights shows up on the eligibility page only.
  • TIP needs a diploma or GED before age 20. This page only knows your age now and the year you finished, so it estimates.
