Free help and where to complain

  • DIFS, Michigan's lending regulator: 877-999-6442, Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Ask whether a lender is licensed, how many payday loans the state database shows for you in the last 12 months, or how to file a complaint. You can also file a complaint online or mail it to DIFS, P.O. Box 30220, Lansing, MI 48909.
  • Check a lender's license with the DIFS licensee locator. Banks and credit unions don't need this license.
  • Michigan Attorney General, Consumer Protection: 877-765-8388. Read its payday loan alert or file a complaint.
  • Consumer Financial Protection Bureau: 855-411-2372 or consumerfinance.gov/complaint. Useful for online lenders that aren't in Michigan.
  • Call 2-1-1 or visit mi211.org for local help with rent, utilities, food, and other bills. For a shutoff or eviction emergency, apply for State Emergency Relief at MI Bridges.
  • Nonprofit credit counseling can help you make a budget and a plan for payday and other debts. The CFPB explains how to find a counselor and what to ask.
  • Free legal help. If a lender or collector is suing you or threatening to, the Michigan Legal Help guide finds free legal aid near you. It also explains how to deal with debt collectors.
  • Military families: find your nearest military legal assistance office.

How this works. The yearly rate (APR) is the fee divided by the amount borrowed, times the number of loan-length stretches in a year (365 divided by the days until it's due). That is the federal Truth in Lending method for a loan paid back in one payment, and it's how the Michigan Attorney General gets 391% for a $15 fee on $100 for 14 days. Michigan's fee cap is 15% of the first $100 you borrow, 14% of the second, 13% of the third, 12% of the fourth, and 11% of the fifth and sixth, plus a database fee of up to $2.40. Green cards are what the rule says directly. Amber cards depend on your facts. Red cards mean act now.
