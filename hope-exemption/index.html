Detroit HOPE Property Tax Exemption Check

Can't pay your property taxes? HOPE, Detroit's Homeowners Property Exemption, is a property tax exemption for homeowners with low income. Michigan calls it the poverty exemption, and the state form is Form 5737. It can lower or wipe out your property taxes, and the city says HOPE, together with Wayne County's PAYS program, has helped thousands of Detroit owners avoid tax foreclosure.

You don't have to be a senior. Neither the state law nor the city's rules set an age requirement, only income limits and an asset limit. Answer a few questions to see whether you likely qualify under the city's 2026 income limits and what your HOPE application needs before the deadline.

This is information, not legal or tax advice. Only Detroit's Board of Review decides who gets HOPE. If you're close to a limit, apply and let the Board decide. Nothing you type here leaves this page or gets saved.

Free help applying

  • Book a free HOPE appointment with the "Schedule a HOPE appointment" button on the city's HOPE page, or at detroithope.timetap.com. The same page lists community groups that help (the HOPE Directory).
  • Questions about the application: call 313-628-0722, or go to the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center, 2 Woodward Ave., Suite 105, Detroit. Both are printed on the city's application.
  • Behind on property taxes: the Wayne County Treasurer is at (313) 224-5990. The Detroit Tax Relief Fund is at (866) 313-2520.
  • Disabled veterans may qualify for a separate exemption. Detroit's Board of Review page covers it.

How this works. The income table, the $12,000 asset limit, the deadline, and the document list come from the City of Detroit's HOPE page and its 2026 HOPE application. The rules behind them are in MCL 211.7u, Michigan's poverty exemption law (last amended by 2023 PA 191). All were checked against the official sources on October 31, 2026.

Income limits are yearly totals for everyone in the home. Income equal to a limit counts as under it, because the city's table lists maximum incomes. The city's asset list says "other real property," so the home you live in isn't counted. It doesn't say whether cars count, so this page doesn't ask about them.

Green cards are what the rules say directly. Amber cards depend on your facts. Red cards mean act now.
