Got an eviction notice, a 7-day Demand for Possession, a Notice to Quit, a summons, a judgment, or an Order of Eviction in Michigan? Pick the paper you're holding and answer a few questions from it. You'll see what the paper means, the dates that come with it, what to look for on it, and what you can do next.

This is legal information, not legal advice. You read the paper and type in what it says. This page doesn't read documents, and it can't know every fact of your case. If a date on your court papers is different from a date here, go by your court papers. Nothing you type leaves this page or gets saved.

3. Notes to bring to legal help

A lawyer or legal aid worker can move faster when they see the paper, the dates, and what you noticed. Bring the papers themselves too, with the envelopes.

Free help

  • Michigan Legal Help: Guide to Legal Help finds free legal aid near you, by county and problem. Court forms also list the State Bar's Lawyer Referral Service, 1-800-968-0738, if you can pay for a lawyer.
  • Michigan Legal Help: Do-It-Yourself Answer to Eviction Complaint asks you questions and fills in the court's answer form for free. Its guides walk through going to court and what happens after court.
  • Detroit tenants: households with income up to twice the federal poverty level can get a free lawyer for an eviction case in 36th District Court. Go to your hearing and tell the judge you want a free lawyer. City of Detroit, Office of Eviction Defense. Detroit Housing Resource helpline: (866) 313-2520.
  • Help paying rent: State Emergency Relief from MDHHS can pay back rent, and court costs in a judgment, to stop an eviction. It asks for a court summons, order, or judgment as proof. Your county's Housing Assessment and Resource Agency (HARA) is the other place the court rules name.
  • Michigan Renter Rights Check on this site covers lockouts, repairs, retaliation, security deposits, and lease clauses.

How this works. The rules come from Michigan's summary proceedings law (MCL 600.5714, 600.5716, 600.5718, 600.5720, 600.5735, 600.5741, 600.5744, and 600.5775), the notice to quit law (MCL 554.134), the lockout law (MCL 600.2918), Michigan Court Rules 4.201, 1.108, and 8.110, and the State Court Administrative Office's landlord-tenant forms (DC 100a, 100b, 100c, 100d, 100e, 102a, 102c, 104, 105, 107, 111a, 111c, 508, 538, and 539). The cards on subsidized housing cite 15 U.S.C. 9058 and 24 CFR 966.4 and 247.4. The rental license card cites Skidmore v Hayashi, a Michigan Court of Appeals opinion from October 21, 2026. All were checked against the official sources on .

Days are calendar days. The day a paper is served or signed isn't counted. Where a court deadline lands on a Saturday, Sunday, or court holiday, the court rule runs it to the end of the next court day, and this page says so when that happens. Your local court can add holidays. Green cards are what the rule says directly. Amber cards depend on your facts. Red cards mean act now.
