Free help

  • Michigan Legal Help: Small Claims. Plain guides for both sides, plus a free Do-It-Yourself Small Claims Suit tool that fills in the court form for you.
  • The district court clerk. The law says the clerk has the small claims forms and must prepare the affidavit for you if you ask. Clerks can explain steps and fees. They can't give legal advice. Find your court in the Michigan trial court directory.
  • Michigan Legal Help: Guide to Legal Help to find free legal aid near you, and the State Bar of Michigan legal aid directory. A lawyer can't speak for you in small claims, but one can still talk with you before you file or before your hearing.
  • Mediation. A trained neutral person helps both sides reach a deal, often for little or no cost. Ask the court clerk, or see Michigan Legal Help's Mediation and Other Forms of Settlement.
  • Can't pay the filing fee? Michigan Legal Help: Fee Waivers in Court Cases.

How this works. The rules come from Chapter 84 of Michigan's Revised Judicature Act (MCL 600.8401 to 600.8427), which sets the $7,000 limit, who can file, where, the fees, and what each side gives up. Court rules MCR 4.301 to 4.306 cover the claim, the hearing, judgments, and removal, with MCR 1.108 for counting days and MCR 2.603 for setting aside a default. The electronic filing fee is MCL 600.1986. Time limits to sue are MCL 600.5805, 600.5807, 600.5813, 440.2725, and 440.3118. A small claims judgment lasts 6 years under MCL 600.5809. The steps and form names come from the State Court Administrative Office's forms, revised June 2026: DC 84 (Small Claims Affidavit), DC 84info, DC 85, DC 85info, and DC 86, and from its District Court Fee and Assessments Table (April 2025). Practical tips marked as such come from Michigan Legal Help. Sources checked November 14, 2026.
