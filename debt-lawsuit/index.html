Sued for a Debt? Answer Deadline

A debt collector, debt buyer, bank, or hospital sued you in a Michigan court and you got a summons and complaint. If you don't answer in writing on time, you lose automatically. Enter the date you were served to see your last day to answer, what happens if you miss it, whether the debt may be too old to sue on, and the steps to respond.

This is information, not legal advice. An answer is a court filing. Use this page to see your dates and options, then use Michigan Legal Help or a legal aid office to prepare what you file. If the court gave you a different date in writing, go by the court's date. Nothing you type here leaves this page or gets saved.

How this works. Deadlines come from the Michigan Court Rules: MCR 2.108 (time to answer), MCR 1.108 (counting days), MCR 2.105 and 2.106 (how papers are served), MCR 2.107 (serving your answer), MCR 2.111 and 2.112 (what goes in an answer and in a debt complaint), MCR 2.603 and 2.612 (default), MCR 2.614 and 3.101 (garnishment), MCR 3.104 (installment payments), MCR 2.002 (fee waivers), MCR 4.301 to 4.306 (small claims), and MCR 8.110 (court holidays). Time limits on old debts come from MCL 600.5807, 440.2725, and 600.5866. Collector conduct rules come from MCL 445.252 and 339.915, the federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (15 U.S.C. 1692 and following), and Regulation F (12 CFR 1006.26). Garnishment limits come from 15 U.S.C. 1673, 42 U.S.C. 407, and 31 CFR Part 212. Court forms are MC 01, MC 03, MC 20, MC 99, MC 13, MC 15, and MC 49.

Sources checked , against the court rules as updated . Days are calendar days. The day you were served is not counted.
