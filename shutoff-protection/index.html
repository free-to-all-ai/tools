Winter Shutoff Protection Check

Behind on your DTE, Consumers Energy, SEMCO, or Michigan Gas Utilities bill, or holding a shut off notice? From November 1 to March 31, the Winter Protection Plan and the rules for seniors stop many shutoffs, and a medical hold works all year. Answer a few questions to see what fits you, about what you'd pay to stay on, what to say when you call, and who can help pay, like MEAP agencies and THAW.

This is information, not legal advice. Your utility decides who qualifies and can ask for proof. If your shutoff notice or your utility's rules give you more time or more protection than this page, go by those. Nothing you type here leaves this page or gets saved.

2. What to say when you call

Your utility won't know you qualify until you tell them. This script follows your answers above. Read it on the phone, or use it as notes.

Your call script

Write down the date, the name of the person you talked to, and any confirmation number. Ask for your enrollment in writing.

Help paying your bill, and help when a utility says no

  • State Emergency Relief (SER) from MDHHS helps with heat and electric bills and can stop a shutoff. Apply at MI Bridges or through your local MDHHS office. Applying counts as seeking heating assistance for the Winter Protection Plan.
  • Call 2-1-1 or visit mi211.org to reach a Michigan Energy Assistance Program (MEAP) agency near you, like THAW (The Heat and Warmth Fund, 800-866-8429), the Salvation Army, or St. Vincent de Paul.
  • MPSC Customer Assistance: 800-292-9555. If a state-regulated utility won't enroll you or follow these rules, call or file a complaint online. A regulated utility can't shut you off while your complaint is open.
  • Home Heating Credit from the Michigan Treasury pays part of winter heating costs for low-income homeowners and renters. You apply between January 1 and September 30. See the MPSC's assistance page for the income limits and a link to the form.

How this works. For state-regulated utilities, the rules come from the Michigan Public Service Commission's Consumer Standards and Billing Practices for Electric and Natural Gas Service, Mich. Admin. Code R 460.101 to R 460.169 (last changed April 10, 2023). City-owned utilities follow state law instead: MCL 460.9q, 460.9r, and 460.9s. Member-regulated co-ops set their own billing rules under MCL 460.36. The military rule is MCL 460.9c. All were checked against the official text on October 31, 2026.

The income line is 150% of the 2026 HHS poverty guidelines (published in the Federal Register on January 15, 2026), which is what the rule points to. The MPSC's own assistance page still showed last season's lower 2025 numbers on October 31, 2026. Payment amounts here are estimates from your own numbers. Your utility works out the real ones.

Is there a new shutoff law? Not as of October 31, 2026. The rules above haven't changed since 2023. House Bill 5047, which would automatically enroll seniors and families with children in winter protection, was still waiting in the House Energy Committee, where it was sent on September 24, 2025.
